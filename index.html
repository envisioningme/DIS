FOREWORD:

The Pakt calls itself a union of the people. Four flags stitched into one banner, four governments that supposedly answer to nothing but the will of their citizens. It is a convenient story, and like most convenient stories, it is not entirely a lie.

Kimnish, Urbian, and Holish did not choose Kushia as their lead member out of admiration. They chose it because Kushia pays for the privilege, and because none of the three have the spine to refuse a seat at a table that keeps them fed. We do not believe the Pakt itself is a hostile actor in the conventional sense.

What concerns this Service is not the Pakt's public charter. It is what moves beneath it.

Every thread we have pulled on the Black Sentinel, every cell, every cache, every uniform stitched from cloth that should not exist outside a CASF quartermaster's stores eventually runs cold at a Kushian border crossing. We cannot yet prove state sponsorship. We can prove that Kushia has never once helped us disprove it.

This file exists to track that gap between what the Pakt says it is and what we suspect it does.

— Deputy Director, DIS

DESCRIPTION

The Pakt of the People is a political and economic alliance comprising the Kimnish Republic, Urbian, and the Holish territories, under the lead membership and primary funding of Kushia. Publicly, the Pakt presents itself as a mutual defense and trade compact formed to counter balance Cascadian and Solstrican regional influence. Its charter guarantees each member state sovereignty over internal affairs. Kushia retains disproportionate control over the Pakt's joint military procurement and intelligence sharing apparatus.

DIS assesses that Kushia's dominant position within the Pakt is not incidental. Kimnish and Urbian lack the industrial base to sustain independent defense procurement; Holish lacks both industry and a standing military of consequence. All three depend on Kushian subsidy to remain usable. This dependency is believed to extend into deniable channels. To reiterate, the movement of material, personnel, and funds toward groups operating outside any member state's declared chain of command.

The Black Sentinel is the primary group of concern. Originating as a volunteer relief network during the Auroran civil wars of the 1890s, its modern incarnation bears no resemblance to that origin beyond the name. DIS has recovered uniform components from Black Sentinel cells on three separate occasions, none of which trace to any confirmed CASF supply chain, active or decommissioned. Financial modeling of recovered Black Sentinel assets consistently points toward Kushian origin, though laundered through enough intermediary shells that no single transaction has yet been made prosecutable or even citable.

OPERATIONAL COMPILATION

Standing DIS protocol for Pakt-linked activity proceeds as follows —

PAKT-01: Any intercepted communication, financial transfer, or material shipment bearing indicators consistent with Black Sentinel activity is flagged and cross referenced against known Pakt state entities, prioritizing Arcadian shell registries.

PAKT-02: Confirmed Black Sentinel cells are surveilled, not neutralized, absent an immediate and demonstrable threat to CASF personnel or Cascadian civilians. Premature disruption has historically severed the financial trail before it reaches its source.

PAKT-03: Assets embedded within Pakt member states report through cutouts only. Direct DIS presence within Arcadia is to be assumed compromised at all times; no operative is to carry material linking them to this file while within borders.

PAKT-04: Any evidence directly implicating a sitting Arcadian official is escalated to the Director and withheld from the Cascadian Federal Intelligence Agency (CFIA) pending Ministerial review.

PAKT-05: All recovered CASF-pattern equipment of unknown provenance is retained for forensic tracing and is never described in public-facing material as confirmed Black Sentinel property, to avoid tipping our evidentiary hand.

INTERROGATION LOG PAKT-0004.1

OPENING STATEMENT: The following is an excerpted transcript of an interview conducted with a detained Black Sentinel courier, apprehended alongside Cell BS-114-K, by DIS Field Officer E. Ripley.

« BEGIN LOG »

RIPLEY: You know where the guns came from.

SUBJECT: I know where I picked them up. Not the same thing.

RIPLEY: Fair enough. Where'd you pick them up?

SUBJECT: A warehouse. Didn't have a flag on it.

RIPLEY: Warehouses have owners.

SUBJECT: So do you, I'd wager. Doesn't mean you know who signs your director's paycheck.

RIPLEY: I know exactly who signs it.

SUBJECT: Lucky you.

RIPLEY sets a photograph of the recovered crates on the table.

RIPLEY: The filing on these serials was sloppy. Rushed. Somebody wanted them moved before somebody else asked questions. That's not how a patient organization operates. That's how a nervous one does.

SUBJECT: Maybe we're nervous.

RIPLEY: About what?

SUBJECT: About people like you asking questions that get closer to an answer you're not ready to have.

RIPLEY: Try me.

SUBJECT: No offense, Officer, but if I tell you and you're not ready for it, you don't do anything useful with it. You just get yourself reassigned, and somebody dumber gets my chair next month.

RIPLEY: And if I am ready for it?

SUBJECT: Then you already know. You're just hoping I'll say it first so it isn't your name on the file.

« END LOG »

CLOSING STATEMENT: Subject provided no further actionable intelligence and was transferred to standard CASF custody following interview. Ripley's report flagged the subject's final statement for Deputy Director review; no further action taken as of file date.

ATTACHED NOTE

We keep waiting for the document that proves it. A signed order, a wire transfer with a Ministry seal on it, anything. It isn't coming. Kushia didn't get this good at deniability by being careless — they got good at it by being patient, and by never needing more than three shell companies to make a trail go cold.

I don't think the other three Pakt states know how deep it runs, or if they do, they've decided it isn't their problem as long as the subsidies keep arriving. That's the part that should worry us more than the guns. A conspiracy is dangerous. A conspiracy nobody wants to look at is worse.

— Deputy Director, DIS
